# Translation Review — rev-08

Targets: `data/translations/story/10_BathHouse.json` (448 strings) and `data/translations/story/11_KokuboSosho.json` (450 strings, story finale).

Method: script-dumped `(section, key, en, th)` triples with fr/de/es/it as second opinions. Reviewed **188 strings in 10** (all 24 `en`>180 chars, all 42 Objective_*/Email*/Briefing_* keys, every 3rd remaining) and **191 in 11** (all 15 long, all 49 Objective_*/Email*/Briefing_* keys, every 3rd remaining) = **379 sampled**, plus full re-dumps of every interrogation section in 11 (~103 strings), all of 10's chat/interrogation sections and all of 11's remaining sections — well over 240 read. Programmatic sweeps run over both files: speaker-label mapping per section, exact/near-duplicate Thai with differing English, Latin-script leakage, term/name variants, rank terms, and length ratio. All flagged pairs were re-read from raw JSON with fr/de/es/it.

## Verdicts

**10_BathHouse.json — 6/10.**
Dialogue prose is strong and glossary names (ฟิชเชอร์/แซม/แลมเบิร์ต/กริมส์ดอตเตียร์/เรดดิง/เชตแลนด์/เรดนิชิน/โคเอน/คาเนดะ) are consistent, but three High defects are real translation failures: two strings carry an entirely different sentence's Thai text (`Speech_0062L`, `Speech_0122L`) and `Speech_0011L` puts Lambert's line under a ฟิชเชอร์ label, inverting a radio exchange. It also loses a point for a negation error that reverses a character's boast and for the file's only structural inconsistency — 58 strings whose speaker label diverges from the English.

**11_KokuboSosho.json — 7/10.**
This is the cleaner file: label handling is 100% consistent (0 divergences across all 450 strings), glossary and rank terms are correct, and the CapturedDialog and every interrogation section read naturally with the taunting/tortured registers intact. It loses points for one garbled sentence that carries no meaning, one doubled word in an on-screen countdown line, and a mistranslated idiom — plus the shipped `TODO` placeholder.

In **10_BathHouse** the label state diverges from English on **58 strings**: `P_10_Bathhouse_Communications` supplies 40 `NAME -` prefixes in `en` but 83 in `th` (43 added where English has none), while `P_10_Bathhouse_ComSummaries` supplies 17 in `en` but only 2 in `th` (15 dropped) — and even inside `ComSummaries` it is not uniform, since `Speech_0007L`/`0008L` keep the label and the other 15 do not. So it is *not* a consistent per-conversation rule; consecutive lines of the same Lambert/Fisher exchange alternate. In **11_KokuboSosho the policy is perfectly consistent: `th` label count equals `en` label count in all 24 sections** (Communications 121/121, CapturedDialog 29/29, InterroHostagers 24/24, GENERAL 3/3, …) — **zero** added or dropped labels across all 450 strings, including the `GENERAL::Briefing_*` and `Speech_0126L–0128L` recaps the brief asked about. The added Thai labels in 10 were spot-checked for correctness and are the right speakers except row 1 (`Speech_0011L`).
- **Shetland/David endgame register (targeted question 1).** The Thai tracks the English beat for beat and keeps the imagery (`จ้องหน้ากันผ่านลำกล้องปืน`, `เธอกำลังตาย` for the feminine "she's dying", `หญิงชราที่ป่วยและตายไปนานแล้ว`). Weight is there, menace is muffled — the single concrete fix is row 10 (`ชื่อเดียว`).
