# Translation Review — rev-07
Targets: `data/translations/story/08_SeoulOne.json` (236 strings), `data/translations/story/09_SeoulTwo.json` (348 strings).
Method: script-dumped `(section, key, en, th)` triples plus fr/de/es/it as second opinions; reviewed **149 strings in 08** (all 14 `en`>180 chars, all Objective/Briefing keys, all 78 interrogation strings, every 3rd remaining) and **220 strings in 09** (all 7 `en`>180, all Objective/Briefing keys, all 124 interrogation strings, every 3rd remaining) — 369 total, well above the 200 minimum. Speaker-label mapping, Latin-script retention, control-character, and length-ratio sweeps were run programmatically over both files.

## Verdicts

**08_SeoulOne.json — 7/10.**
Mission dialogue reads accurately and naturally, and glossary names (ฟิชเชอร์/แซม/แลมเบิร์ต/กริมส์ดอตเตียร์/NKA/เธิร์ดเอเชลอน) are consistent. It loses points for a fabricated speaker label for `MP_One`, one register/word-choice error ("ดุร้าย"), and 15 subtitles that drop a speaker label the English transcript always carries.

**09_SeoulTwo.json — 8/10.**
Prose quality is the stronger of the two: interrogations read naturally, the P3R/MCC/UAV/EEV acronym policy is consistently Latin-script, and the Chung interrogation's taunting register lands well. Defects concentrate in three spots — leftover English ("roadside", "TODO"), one real semantic error (`ทุพพลภาพ`), and a corrupted source character still sitting in `Objective_0050`.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Glossary compliance is good.** ฟิชเชอร์ / แซม / แลมเบิร์ต / กริมส์ดอตเตียร์ / เธิร์ดเอเชลอน all render per the locked glossary, and `NKA`, `NDT`, `EA-6B`, `P3R`, `UAV`, `MCC`, `EEV`, `NASA`, `WNM`, `NATO`, `IFV` are consistently Latin-script. The only Latin leakage found in the entire 584-string set is `roadside` ×2 and `TODO` ×1.
- **`Jong` is always `จง` (11 strings), never the locked `จอง ปอมชู`** — introduce the locked full form once, then shorten.
- **`I-SDF` appears in neither file** as Latin; the Japanese force is spelled out `กองกำลังป้องกันตนเองญี่ปุ่น` (3 strings). If other revs use an I-SDF initialism, add a glossary line to prevent a cross-file split.
- **No length blowouts**: no Thai string exceeds 3x its English length + 30 chars.
- **Speaker-label integrity**: 15 strings in 08 (12 × `FISHER` across the two `Interro_Elite*` sections, plus 3 `Briefings_Texts` entries missing `LAMBERT`/`GRIMSDOTTIR`/`REDDING`) and 1 in 09 (`Interog_Shing::Speech_0001L`, missing `FISHER`) drop a `NAME - ` prefix that `en`/`fr` carry and that sibling strings in the same section keep. The `_Interro_Elite*` FISHER omissions are the most visible, since the reply lines still open with a Thai speaker label.

## Three highest-impact fixes

1. **Replace the fabricated `MP_One` label `สายลับทหารหนึ่ง` ("spy") with a real MP/officer label** (row 1) — 6 strings in 08_SeoulOne, and it misidentifies an allied character as an enemy intelligence agent in a mission-critical interrogation.
2. **Fix `เพื่อช่วยทุพพลภาพเกาหลีเหนือ` in `09_SeoulTwo_Fin::Objective_0002`** (row 11) — on-screen objective text that says the opposite of what it means.
3. **Purge leftover English: `roadside` ×2 in the Chung interrogation and `TODO` in `09_SeoulTwo::GENERAL::Briefing`** (rows 12, 17) — the clearest failures of the localization contract, with `roadside` sitting mid-line inside a heated interrogation.
